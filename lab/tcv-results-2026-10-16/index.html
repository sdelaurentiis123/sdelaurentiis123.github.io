Matched objectives

Balanced loss: equal normalized contributions from toroidal mean and fluctuations. Local covariance loss: auxiliary matching of local cross-field second moments. Joint-patch distribution loss: auxiliary distribution matching for multifield patch vectors; it does not guarantee that every reconstructed structure is in the correct place.

2. What the fluctuation investigation resolved

Two different questions: how much fluctuation is present, and whether the model reconstructs the actual fluctuation at the right place. The first can be right while the second is wrong.

QuantityWhat it asksWhat it does not establish
Fluctuation RMSHow large is q′ = q − instantaneous toroidal mean?Whether model and truth have the same structures
Power spectrumHow is fluctuation variance distributed across toroidal modes?Correct phase or position
Cross-phaseHow are two fields aligned within a mode?Alignment of each field with truth; shared shifts can preserve cross-phase
Local cross-phase errorDoes the field-pair phase match truth at each location before averaging?Correct amplitudes or a complete transport budget
Fluctuation RMSEHow different are model q′ and truth q′ at corresponding cells?By itself, whether amplitude or phase caused that difference

Four fresh seed-0 denoisers, 16,384 updates each, same frozen short-data codec; 32 DDIM sampling steps. Evaluation: 105 overlapping chronological validation starts, one sampled path per start, leads 1–16 (about 3.13–50.11 µs). These are not ensemble-calibration results. The physical-loss and history-noise arms are separate changes to the forecast-conditioned control.

Forecast performance through time

Forecast conditioning improved the first-step result and retained more power. History noise reduced integrated particle error but worsened target-heat error relative to clean forecast conditioning. No one recipe wins every observable. The new long-data and block/rolling tests have not yet produced results.

Definitions used throughout

TermDefinition
Five fieldsNe: electron density (m⁻³); Te/Ti: electron/ion temperature (eV); phi: electrostatic potential (V); Vi: parallel ion velocity (m/s). Plot axes may rescale density or velocity.
Mean and fluctuationsq₀ is the instantaneous toroidal mean; q′=q−q₀. This is not subtraction of a fixed time average.
Ordinary physical MSENative-volume-weighted squared physical field error, normalized by fitting-only field variance.
Balanced objectiveEqual normalized mean and fluctuation error contributions; each uses its fitting-only component scale with a standard-deviation floor.
GradNormAdaptive loss weights using gradient magnitudes/training rates; the patch objective learns component weights, not a new physical field.
RMSE / relative errorRMSE=sqrt(weighted mean squared error). Relative L2=RMS(error)/RMS(truth) on the stated support. Percentages are not percent of cells wrong.
RMSE skill1−model RMSE / fitting-mean-baseline RMSE. Zero matches baseline; negative is worse. Saved correlations are accompanied by this skill.
Toroidal k and nFourier bin k; physical mode n=5k for the72° domain. n=0 is mean; resolved nonzero n=5…200.
Cross-phasearg(q̂a · conjugate(q̂b)). Error is the wrapped model–truth angle difference. Local and pooled operations are not interchangeable.
Transport versus heatRadial E×B particle/internal-energy transport and recovered divertor sheath heat are different operators on different spatial supports.
Clean replayA full-input reconstruction loss added during sparse-observation adaptation; coefficient0.5 is not a50% example fraction.
Autoregression / diffusionAutoregression feeds predictions back through time. Diffusion denoises a sampled target and can itself run autoregressively.
Full-target / residualFull-target generates the future latent. Residual generates a correction to a deterministic forecast. Forecast conditioning alone does not make a residual model.
