Coordinates, colors and scoring protocol

Vertical: native radial index x=16 at the bottom to x=63 at the top. Larger x goes farther into the scrape-off layer. These are cell addresses, not metres. Horizontal: toroidal sample centers ζ=0–71.11° in a periodic 72° wedge. Ben’s raw radial indices include two guards: 18–65 are our 16–63. Selecting inner/outer changes the poloidal slice y, not the radial axis.

Every target retains all 48×81 native cells. Smooth view evaluates their periodic Fourier series at eight display samples per toroidal cell, with radial rows unchanged and no alignment shift. Native view remains available. Both views use identical scores; smooth interpolation can overshoot. Target truth and prediction share one fixed color scale across the 48 displayed frames. Residual uses the same magnitude limit on a signed scale. Fluctuation limits are the 99th percentile of displayed truth amplitude; absolute-field limits use its maximum. Saturated cells are counted under each image. Inputs have separately labeled scales.

Full-field errors use all scored frames and equal weight per native target cell; changing the color transformation never changes those scores. The toroidally varying score removes the instantaneous toroidal mean of each training-mean anomaly (n≠0), with its own zero-anomaly reference. These inspected trajectories do not establish transfer to an independent plasma condition.

Did the outer leg become more connected?

The stronger inner-leg match remains in this record after the checked mapping. Temperature is more connected than density, and the varying-pattern peak remains near zero sampled delay. Those are measured relationships; the map check does not establish the physical cause of the asymmetry.

Why a lag peak is not a sound-speed measurement

Ben’s distinction matters: advection and conduction/diffusion act together, and conditions vary along the field line. A local target sound speed is not a path-integrated transit model. These passive correlation curves do not measure an imposed perturbation’s travel time. The n=0 component is the toroidal average and can still evolve in time; it is not automatically a static equilibrium.

The physics and the models, simply

A tokamak confines plasma with a twisting magnetic field. Outside the confinement boundary, open field lines lead to the divertor targets. The plasma can change along those lines through flows, heat transport, sources and losses; a connected line need not carry an unchanged image.

Experiment 1: compare actual upstream and target fluctuations after accounting for magnetic geometry. Experiment 2: learn from earlier upstream/target pairs, then predict later target fields using only upstream observations. These movies reconstruct each observed instant; they do not integrate a complete plasma state forward in time.

The models: a magnetic map copies a pattern with the correct twist; a fitted gain also changes its amplitude. Fourier ridge learns how upstream radial profiles combine into downstream profiles, separately at each toroidal wavelength. GAOT learns a nonlinear mapping. The controlled comparison tests whether its coordinate correction improves prediction under the same fitting and scoring protocol.

Next: use the verified comparison and errors by radial row and wavelength to choose the next change, then define a physically meaningful camera footprint and measurement before claiming camera performance.
