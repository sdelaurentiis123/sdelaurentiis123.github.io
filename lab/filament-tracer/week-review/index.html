What can an upstream patch tell us about the divertor?

Nearby sections are predictable. Reconstructing the whole leg from one observation is harder. Ridge remains a strong reference; longer attention training and small-window curricula have not established an advantage.

3. Longer attention training did not resolve the endpoint gap

All eight first-step arms now have completed training records. High-band endpoint temperature MAE spans 0.339–0.360 eV, versus 0.226 eV for same-band direct ridge. Low-band attention spans 0.950–0.996 eV, versus 0.910 eV for ridge. A pass cap is not proof of convergence.

How to read the evidence

MAE is average absolute native-cell error; RMSE penalizes large errors more. These scores aggregate all 648 evaluation frames, not one movie frame. The fitting-mean reference, toroidally varying error and dynamic amplitude distinguish background reconstruction from turbulence. A smooth movie can have a good background and still miss moving structures.

Fit frames: 0–975. Validation: 1006–1257. Inspected regression: 1288–1935. Both 30-frame gaps remain. Results are exploratory on one record and one seed, with unequal model compute. No independent-shot generalization or transport-law discovery follows.

The completed figures support useful local relationships, strong dependence on radial context, and an unresolved long-range reconstruction problem. They do not establish that the plasma is linear, that shear alone causes the gap, or that more training must beat ridge.
